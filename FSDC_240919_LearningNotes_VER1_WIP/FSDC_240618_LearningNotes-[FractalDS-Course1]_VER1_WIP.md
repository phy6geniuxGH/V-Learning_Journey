### Solving Problems Like a Pro
- The Seven-step problem solving process
    - “What are we trying to solve? What are the constraints that exist? What are the dependencies?”  OK. So step one—and there is a real art and a structure to it—is define the problem.
    - My favorite step is step two, which is to use logic trees to disaggregate the problem. 
    - third step in the process, which is that you have to prioritize. which we also emphasize, along with good problem definition, is rigorous prioritization—we ask the questions “How important is this lever or this branch of the tree in the overall outcome that we seek to achieve? How much can I move that lever?” We try and focus our efforts on ones that have a big impact on the problem and the ones that we have the ability to change
    - You’ve defined your problem, you disaggregate it, you prioritize where you want to analyze—what you want to really look at hard. Then you got to the work plan. Now, what does that mean in practice? You need to find a work plan that reflects the level of precision, the time frame you have, and the stakeholders you need to bring along in the exercise. We call it the one-day answer or the one-hour answer. In work planning, we’re always iterating. Be careful of biases, like cognitive, availability, bias based on hierarchies (sunflower bias) a cognitive bias in which people tend to agree with the most dominant or assertive person in a group. In the same way, people with sunflower bias tend to follow the lead of the most dominant person in a group, even if they disagree with them.
    - Let’s move on to step five. You’ve done your work plan. Now you’ve actually got to do the analysis. : For me, the most important thing is that we start with simple heuristics and explanatory statistics before we go off and use the big-gun tools. We need to understand the shape and scope of our problem before we start applying these massive and complex analytical approaches. . Your problem-solving methodology is test and learn, test and learn, test and learn, and iterate. That is a heuristic in itself, the A/B testing that is used in many parts of the world. So that’s a problem-solving methodology. It’s nothing different. It just uses technology and feedback loops in a fast way.  The other one is exploratory data analysis. When you’re dealing with a largescale problem, and there’s so much data, I can get to the heuristics that Charles was talking about through very clever visualization of data. You’re testing, you’re checking—“Is the data right? Is it sound? Does it make sense?”—before you launch too far?
    - Step six. You’ve done your analysis. The ability to synthesize the pieces that came out of the analysis and begin to weave those into a story that helps people answer the question “What should I do?” If we can’t synthesize, and we can’t tell a story, then our decision maker can’t find the answer to “What should I do?”
    - “This is a likely answer under the following sets of conditions” and then make the level of uncertainty clearer, if that is appropriate. : “Acknowledge and embrace the uncertainty. Don’t pretend that it isn’t there. Be very clear about what the uncertainties are up front, and then build that into every step of the process.”

### Common Pitfalls
- The Loop Trap
    - To avoid:
        - Define the problem statement as accurately as possible
        - What is the issue you are trying to solve
        - What are the symptoms?
        - What might be causing them?
- The Complexity Trap
    - To avoid:
        - Define problem scope, narrow the scope
        - Identify root cause, primary cause
        - Prioritize, Develop target solution
        - Systems thinking, streamline strategies and efforts
        - Collabortation
- The Obvious Trap
    - To avoid:
        - Challenge assumptions
            - Avoid limiting problem-solving approach
        - Gather information
            - Identify underlying factors
        - Look beyond surface-level issues
            - Identify the root causes
        - Consider consequences
            - develop effective solutions
        - Collaborate
            - Uncover hidden issues and identify blind spots
- Collaboration is the key:
    - Holistic Understanding: Different viewpoints provide a more complete picture of the problem.

    - Uncovering Biases: Diverse perspectives help identify and challenge implicit biases.

    - Innovation: Varied backgrounds foster creative and innovative solutions.

    - Buy-In: Inclusion of different perspectives increases engagement and collaboration.

    - Comprehensive Problem Statements: Ensures all relevant factors are considered.

    - Resilience: Diverse teams are more adaptable to change and challenges.

    - Impact Assessment: Anticipates the effects on a broader range of stakeholders.

- Defining a Problem Statement

Problem: Frame a problem statement using the SMART framework to address the issue of improving energy efficiency in a residential setting.  

Specific: To efficiently consume energy in a reduce manner, in a residential setting. Measurable: Decrease the energy consumption by 50% within 1 year, and compare it to the previous 5-year average. Achievable: Use inverter-type appliances, apply AI-enabled devices for optimized energy consumption, turn off unused appliances, implementing day-night cycle for appliances that require day-night operations. Relevant: Reduction of expenses due to electricity, attain greener use of energy, and contribute to lessen the global warming due to increase used of energy-hungry appliances and practices Time-bound: achieve the reduced energy consumption within 1 year. 

Problem Statement: To address the problem of high-residential energy consumption, we implement energy-reduction practices, that can be achieved within a year with a 50% decrease in consumption when compared to a previous 5-year consumption average. Usual residential households can 10,500 kilowatthours (kWh) of electricity per year , although it varies depending on the socioeconomic status, the energy consumption practices, and the efficiency of the house to utilize the energy. However, we can try to reduce the energy consumption through the ways we can control. The practices that can help in energy-reduction involve the use of inverter-type appliances, application of AI-enabled devices, the turn-off unnecessary appliances, and day-night use cycles. These steps can further help to lessen the global warming due to carbon emissions and excess production of wasted heat. The proposed steps aim to accomplish the optimization and reduction of energy consumption within 1 year.
